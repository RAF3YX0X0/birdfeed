Why restaurants struggle

What makes social hard for restaurants.

  1. 01

    Posting dies on busy weeks

    The weeks you’re slammed are exactly when your feed goes quiet, and the algorithm punishes the gap.

  2. 02

    Phone photos undersell the food

    Great dishes look average with rushed photos and no design, and average doesn’t stop a scroll.

  3. 03

    Specials never reach locals

    Events, seasonal menus, and slow-night promos flop when nobody sees them in time.

An example month

A month on your feed.

What a month of posts could look like for a restaurant like yours. We plan yours around your offers, your seasons and your goals; your plan sets how many posts you get.

Week 1

  1. MonReel

    Dish of the week, plated in slow motion

    Food Reels get saved and shared, which is how nearby diners find you.

  2. WedCarousel

    This week’s specials

    Gives regulars a reason to come back this week, not someday.

  3. FriStory

    Weekend booking reminder with a link

    Catches people deciding where to eat tonight.

Week 2

  1. MonReel

    Behind the pass: Friday service in 15 seconds

    The energy of a full room is the best ad for a table.

  2. WedCarousel

    Meet the chef: where the menu comes from

    People book places with a face and a story.

  3. FriPhoto

    Guest photo of the week (with their OK)

    Real guests make the next guest feel welcome.

Week 3

  1. MonReel

    How we make our signature dish

    Process videos hold attention and build appetite.

  2. WedCarousel

    What to drink with what: a pairing guide

    Useful posts get saved, and saved posts get seen again.

  3. FriStory

    Poll: which dish should come back?

    Two taps of engagement that keep you in their feed.

Week 4

  1. MonReel

    Our regulars’ favourite order

    Social proof in the form of food.

  2. WedPhoto

    Private dining or event announcement

    Fills the slower nights and bigger tables.

  3. FriCarousel

    Your reviews, our thanks

    Shows happy guests and thanks them in public.
