Different formats.
Different jobs in the funnel.

Not every ad creative does the same thing. Knowing where each format sits helps you allocate budget smarter, and tells you which format to reach for when a campaign is underperforming.

Awareness
Cold audiences, they don't know you yet
Consideration
Warm audiences, seen you, not converted yet
Conversion
Hot audiences, ready to buy or close to it
Video Ads
Introduce, demonstrate, tell the story
UGC Videos
Social proof, trust, authentic recommendation
Static Ads
Direct response, clear offer, drive the click
Top of funnel, Awareness

Video Ads

Cold audiences meet your brand here. A great vertical video introduces, demonstrates, and builds your warm retargeting pool while it runs.

•Best for cold audiences and broad top-of-funnel campaigns
•Ideal when your product needs context before a buying decision
•Feeds warm retargeting audiences for lower-funnel ads
Mid funnel, Consideration

UGC Videos

Looks and reads like an editorial piece, but guides the reader toward your product. Great for people not yet ready to buy when they first click.

•Best for warm retargeting audiences who need a trust signal
•Also strong at top of funnel in social-proof-driven categories
•Pairs naturally with video ads, introduce the brand, then prove it
Bottom of funnel, Conversion

Static Ads

Formatted like a "top 5" or "best of" article. Feels like editorial content rather than an ad, so visitors read more and trust more before your offer.

•Best for retargeting and high-intent audiences
•Fastest and most cost-effective format for creative volume testing
•Built for offers, promotions, and direct calls to action

In practice, all three work together. Video ads introduce you and build warm audiences. UGC converts the consideration layer. Static ads close the retargeting pool. Running all three gives you full-funnel coverage, and gives Meta's algorithm the creative diversity it needs to find buyers at every stage.

What separates good creative from great creative

The principles we apply to every ad we make.

These are not opinions. They come from studying thousands of high-performing ads across every category, and they inform every brief we write and every asset we deliver.

The first three seconds

The hook is not a headline. It is the entire ad.

Research consistently shows users decide whether to keep scrolling within the first one to two seconds. Our copywriters work from tested hook frameworks, and we develop multiple hook variants per creative so you can test which angle resonates fastest.

Problem-reveal hooks for high-awareness audiences
Curiosity-gap hooks for cold traffic
Social proof opens for mid-funnel
Contrast and transformation for product demos
Bold direct-claim hooks for purchase intent
Native-style openers for UGC placements
Graphic design for paid social

Designed to interrupt. Built to hold attention.

Good ad design is intentionally disruptive, high-contrast compositions that feel different from the organic content surrounding them.

Layouts that direct the eye toward the key message before anything else registers.

Typography weighted and sized for silent-view environments where someone sees the text before they hear the audio.

Video editing as performance strategy

Pacing, captions, and sound design as conversion levers.

Sharp cuts that maintain attention across the full run time.

Bold on-screen subtitles that carry the message clearly when sound is off, which is how most people watch.

Music and sound design chosen to reinforce the emotional tone of the hook, with motion graphics that emphasise the key benefit at the right moment.
